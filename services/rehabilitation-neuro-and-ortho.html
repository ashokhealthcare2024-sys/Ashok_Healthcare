Clinical Scope: Neuro vs. Orthopedic Rehabilitation

Service FocusPossible indications and interventions
Neuro rehabilitation at home
  • Post-stroke hemiplegia, weakness, and paralysis recovery support.
  • Parkinson's disease, ALS, and other neuromuscular disorders, subject to individual assessment.
  • Traumatic brain injury or spinal cord trauma recovery support.
  • Balance retraining, gait practice, and speech/swallowing concerns coordinated with the appropriate clinician.
Orthopedic rehabilitation at home
  • Mobility practice after total knee replacement (TKR) or total hip replacement (THR).
  • Recovery after complex fractures, following the surgeon's weight-bearing restrictions.
  • Joint mobility and scar care support as directed by the treating team.
  • Spine-related mobility needs, including lumbar spondylosis or cervical disc conditions, following medical assessment.

The appropriate therapy, frequency, and goals depend on the patient's condition, assessment, precautions, and treating clinician's advice. Rehabilitation outcomes and timelines vary between individuals.

Individualized Recovery Goals & Progress Tracking

The treating therapist may track mobility, joint range with a goniometer, balance, strength, and daily function using appropriate measures such as the Barthel Index. The example goals below are planning references only—not promised milestones, timelines, or outcomes. A therapist sets appropriate targets after assessment and in coordination with the treating physician.

Review periodExamples of neuro-rehabilitation goalsExamples of orthopedic goals

Weeks 3–4 · sub-acute phasePossible goals include active-assisted standing balance, upper-limb reach/grasp practice, and tracking functional change with an appropriate measure.Possible goals include progression of knee flexion (sometimes toward 90°–110°) and safe walking practice with the prescribed aid.
Weeks 5–8 · functional phasePossible goals include gait, hand-eye coordination, and daily activity practice according to individual progress.Possible goals may include further knee range, stairs, and outdoor mobility when cleared by the treating team.

Session Formats & Rehabilitation Packages

Session formats

  • Standard one-to-one sessions are typically 45–60 minutes, subject to therapist assessment and availability.
  • Two visits per day may be considered for selected post-operative or early stroke recovery plans when clinically appropriate and available.
  • Frequency and duration are individualized with the treating team.

Example care packages

  • A 14-day post-surgical knee plan may include daily home physiotherapy visits, CPM coordination when prescribed, dressing coordination, and range-of-motion tracking.
  • A 30-day neuro-rehabilitation plan may include scheduled neuro-physiotherapy visits, speech assessment coordination, nursing support, and repositioning guidance where appropriate.
  • Package contents, schedule, equipment, clinical suitability, and charges must be confirmed with the care team before booking.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How soon after a stroke or knee replacement should home rehabilitation start?

Rehabilitation should begin only after the treating doctor has medically cleared the patient. Timing varies with the patient's condition and discharge instructions; the team can review those instructions and discuss an assessment, often soon after discharge if clinically appropriate.

2. What is the difference between general physiotherapy and neuro physiotherapy?

General physiotherapy often addresses musculoskeletal pain, injury, mobility, or post-operative needs. Neuro physiotherapy focuses on movement, balance, coordination, and function affected by neurological conditions, tailored to individual assessment.

3. Can we rent a CPM machine for post-TKR knee rehabilitation at home?

A CPM machine may be available through Ashok Surgical, subject to stock and clinical suitability. Confirm availability and rental terms; use the machine only according to the treating surgeon or physiotherapist's instructions.

4. What happens if our assigned physiotherapist is unavailable?

Contact the Yeshwanthpur hub to discuss scheduling and a suitable replacement if available. Replacement timing depends on the patient's needs, location, and therapist availability.

5. Do your physiotherapists bring their own rehabilitation equipment to home sessions?

Portable equipment depends on the therapist's assessment and the patient's plan. Larger equipment such as CPM machines or parallel bars may be arranged separately through Ashok Surgical, subject to availability.
