Hospital-Grade Rehabilitation Delivered at Home

Home-based rehabilitation can help some patients practice recovery activities in a familiar setting, while avoiding the need to travel for every session. Suitability depends on medical stability, the clinician's plan, home safety, and availability of qualified professionals.

Physical therapy

A therapist may work on strength, balance, transfers, walking, or range of motion according to the recovery plan.

Daily living skills

Occupational therapy may address daily activities and practical ways to adapt the home environment.

Speech and swallowing

When recommended, a qualified speech-language professional can assess communication or swallowing needs.

Nursing and step-down support

Nursing or equipment support may be coordinated for patients who need it as part of the clinician-approved home plan.

Home services do not replace hospital care when inpatient or emergency treatment is needed. For local neuro and orthopedic programs, see neuro and orthopedic rehabilitation.

Specialized In-Home Rehabilitation Programs

Stroke and neurological rehabilitation

Rehabilitation may address strength, balance, walking, coordination, and everyday function after a stroke or neurological condition.

  • Movement and transfer practice suited to the patient's ability.
  • Balance and gait activities when clinically appropriate.
  • Coordination with occupational or speech-language therapy if recommended.

Orthopedic surgery and joint replacement

After joint replacement, fracture, or spine surgery, the treating team may prescribe progressive rehabilitation.

  • Range-of-motion and strengthening within post-operative limits.
  • Mobility and weight-bearing guidance as prescribed.
  • Fall-risk awareness and safe transfer practice.

Geriatric balance and fall prevention

Older adults may benefit from support tailored to mobility, confidence, and safety needs.

  • Functional movement and balance activities.
  • Home hazard review and safer transfer planning.
  • Coordination with family and clinicians about daily routines.

Cardiopulmonary and post-ICU recovery

Some patients need clinician-directed breathing or mobility rehabilitation after respiratory illness or critical care.

  • Breathing and activity plans recommended by the care team.
  • Monitoring and escalation instructions where prescribed.
  • Coordination with nursing or respiratory support if indicated.

Exercise selection, intensity, and progression must be determined by an appropriate clinician or therapist. Explore home physiotherapy and discuss the right referral with the treating doctor.

Clinical and Emergency Support Services

Depending on the patient's assessment and plan, home rehabilitation may need coordination with other services:

  • Home ICU support: Where a treating clinician considers home critical care appropriate, the equipment, trained staffing, power backup, and escalation plan must be reviewed. See home ICU setup services.

Care Coordination and Recovery Planning

Before home therapy begins, share relevant discharge instructions with the care team—with the patient's consent—and agree on a practical plan.

  1. Review the clinical plan: Confirm precautions, weight-bearing status, wound instructions, and any limits on activity with the treating clinician.
  2. Set functional goals: Discuss the patient's priorities, such as transfers, walking indoors, or managing daily activities.
  3. Assess the home: Identify stairs, narrow passages, bathroom access, and equipment needs.
  4. Coordinate roles: Clarify what the therapist, nurse, attendant, and family members each do.
  5. Review progress: Agree how concerns or changes in function are reported to the family and treating team.
