Bridging the Gap Between Hospital Discharge and Home Recovery

When a loved one leaves hospital, recovery continues at home. Post-operative care, ongoing clinical needs, and daily support for older adults can be challenging for families. Home healthcare can provide support in familiar surroundings when it is appropriate for the patient's condition and consistent with their treating clinician's instructions.

Core Clinical Services

1. Professional home nursing care

Nursing support may be arranged in shifts according to assessment and availability. Procedures must be prescribed or directed as appropriate and performed by professionals qualified for the task.

  • Wound and stoma care according to clinical instructions.
  • Prescribed injections or infusions within the nurse's scope.
  • Catheter, feeding-tube, and tracheostomy care when qualified and included in the plan.
  • Observations and care records as agreed with the treating team.

2. Home ICU and critical-care coordination

Home critical care is appropriate only for selected patients following a clinical assessment and a treating clinician's plan.

  • Review equipment and staffing needs before setup.
  • Confirm caregiver training and the equipment handover process.
  • Agree on power continuity and urgent escalation arrangements.
  • Clarify ongoing clinical supervision and replacement procedures.

3. Condition-specific rehabilitation

Therapy plans should be tailored by qualified professionals and coordinated with the patient's doctor.

  • Stroke and neurological recovery: Movement, balance, and daily-function goals.
  • Orthopedic recovery: Mobility and exercise within post-operative precautions.
  • Geriatric support: Safe mobility, balance, and everyday routines.
  • Cardiopulmonary recovery: Clinician-directed activity and breathing support when appropriate.

4. Senior, dementia, and palliative support

Care can focus on dignity, comfort, daily living, and family coordination.

  • Assistance with hygiene and daily activities.
  • Support with routines and safe mobility.
  • Companionship and family communication.
  • Comfort-focused support aligned with the care team's advice.

Home Onboarding Process

  1. Initial discussion: Share the patient's current needs, location, and expected start date.
  2. Care assessment: Review relevant discharge documents and prescriptions with the patient's consent.
  3. Care plan: Agree on duties, schedule, equipment, escalation contacts, and family communication.
  4. Caregiver matching: Confirm the assigned professional's qualifications and experience for the agreed tasks.
  5. Review and follow-up: Ask how progress, concerns, and changes to the plan will be communicated.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Are home nurses qualified and background-verified?

Ask the provider to confirm the assigned professional's GNM or B.Sc Nursing qualification, applicable registration, experience, and screening checks before care begins.

2. What happens if the assigned nurse takes leave or falls sick?

Ask about backup staffing, expected replacement timing, any additional fee, and how clinical handover is managed. Get commitments in writing.

3. How does pricing work for 12-hour vs. 24-hour nursing care?

Pricing depends on the caregiver's qualifications, patient needs, staffing arrangement, and inclusions. Request an itemized quote and clarify rest periods, replacement coverage, consumables, and extra charges.

4. How quickly can a Home ICU or oxygen concentrator be set up?

Timing varies with clinical assessment, address, equipment availability, and staffing. Confirm current timing directly, and use equipment only as directed by the treating clinician.
